# Replication Attempt: Woodstock, Vermont

Run September 1-2, 2026, against the gate in
[REPLICATION_BLUEPRINT.md](REPLICATION_BLUEPRINT.md).

**Result: Woodstock did not clear the gate. No Woodstock page, record, or civic
claim was published.** This document exists because a failed gate is a result,
not a dead end, and the next person to attempt this town should start from what
was already established rather than repeat it.

## Why this attempt happened

The blueprint stated a seven-step bar for adding a municipality but had never
been executed against a second town. "We wrote the gate but never ran it" is a
weaker claim than either passing it or failing it honestly. Woodstock was
selected by the owner. Every source below was read from an official page; no
fact here was inferred, and nothing was accepted from a search-engine summary.

## What passed

**Municipal structure — established, and load-bearing.** The Town of Woodstock
and the Village of Woodstock are two distinct, currently active municipal
entities. The applicable municipal tax rate depends on whether a parcel sits
inside the Village: village parcels carry a `VILLAGE` rate line, non-village
parcels carry a `TOWN POLICE` line instead, and the remaining municipal lines
(general, highway, local agreement, special articles, county) are common to
both. The town's own prior-year annual report names the same mechanism in
words — "Police Assessment (Non-village property)" — confirming a standing
structural feature rather than a one-year artifact. The Board of Village
Trustees is currently seated with terms running through 2027-2029.

Consequence for any future attempt: a single flat "Woodstock municipal rate"
would be wrong for a large share of the town. The village boundary is a
publishable condition, never a footnote.

**Three of four contact destinations corroborated across two independent
official document types:**

| Office | Second source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Town Assessors | The Assessors' own grievance-application letterhead PDF | Corroborated |
| Town Clerk | Vermont Secretary of State clerks and treasurers guide, updated January 2026 | Corroborated |
| Town Treasurer (elected office) | Town annual report elected-officials page, plus the same state guide | Corroborated |

## Why it failed

**1. The 2026 rates are only capturable from individual residents' tax bills.**
No published FY2026 summary rate table was found on the town's site. The rates
exist in per-parcel bills that identify named owners. This product already
withholds bulk owner-record destinations from its entire deployed artifact for
Waterbury; sourcing a headline published fact from three named residents' tax
bills would invert that boundary. The owner-record URLs are deliberately not
recorded in this document for the same reason.

**2. The bills do not print their units.** Reading "per $100 of assessed value"
into the numbers requires applying standard Vermont grand-list convention. That
is an inference, and this product does not infer. A rate published without
captured units is either unusable or misleading.

**3. The town's own 2026 grievance application contradicts itself on the
deadline.** One sentence reads "Submit application after April 1 and before the
deadline of June 12th 2026." A later sentence in the same official PDF reads
"All Grievance Applications must be in writing and submitted via email or mail
by the close of grievance day, June 14th." Two days apart, one document, no
wording that distinguishes a submission date from a postmark date. Publishing
either date would state as fact something the town itself has not settled.

**4. The working tax-billing contact is single-source.** The office a resident
would actually reach about a bill appears on one page of the town's own site,
named by neither the annual report nor the state directory. The elected
Treasurer is corroborated, but the town's own site directs billing questions
elsewhere, so publishing only the corroborated destination risks a routing that
the town does not itself use.

**Also unresolved, lower stakes:** the town says the Village was chartered in
1836; the Vermont Secretary of State's incorporated-villages list says 1837.
State records additionally show a 1981 merger action recorded as repealed. A
selectboard merger-analysis process was reported in 2026 by a newspaper — a
secondary source, and an open process, which is not publishable as a pending
merger.

## What a human would have to do to close it

1. Ask the Assessors' office or the Finance office to resolve the June 12 versus
   June 14, 2026 grievance deadline and supply one citable date.
2. Get the town to state, for public-facing purposes, whether a resident with a
   tax-bill question should reach the elected Treasurer or the Finance office.
3. Obtain or locate a published FY2026 rate table, with units, that is not a
   named resident's bill.
4. Ask which charter year the town considers authoritative.

Items 1, 2, and 4 require contacting the town. Nothing in this project contacts
anyone on a resident's behalf, and that applies to its own research too — these
are calls a person makes.

## The conclusion worth keeping

Woodstock is not a poorly documented town. It publishes more, and more
structurally interesting material, than most of the ten other Vermont
municipalities surveyed alongside it — none of which cleared the bar either.
Two of those ten return HTTP 403 to automated retrieval on their own official
sites. The Vermont Department of Taxes assessor certification list, the obvious
second source for a contact, was found to contain malformed rows and at least
one assessor tied to the wrong town, and was therefore discarded as a standalone
corroboration source partway through this attempt.

The gate did what it was built to do. The failure modes it caught were a privacy
boundary, an inferred unit, a self-contradicting official document, and an
uncorroborated destination — every one of them a thing that would have reached a
real resident as a confident, wrong answer.
