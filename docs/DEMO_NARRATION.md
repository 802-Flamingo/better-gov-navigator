# Demo Narration

Target: about two minutes at a calm speaking pace.

Property-tax questions look simple, but public rates cannot explain an
individual bill. Go Vermont Civic Navigator helps a Waterbury resident separate
what official records establish from what still requires an accountable human
answer.

The experience works without an assistant. It is independent civic information,
not a municipal website, and it does not diagnose a bill or calculate tax.

Here I enter a synthetic question about an assessed value. The page immediately
shows the privacy boundary: no account numbers, Social Security numbers, payment
details, or other sensitive information.

Next, the Navigator shows Waterbury's published 2026 rates, the official sources
behind every statement, and the limits of those records. It also names the facts
that remain unknown, including whether the assessed value or property
classification changed.

I choose "Question an assessed value." That choice maps to one reviewed starting
point: the Waterbury assessor. The page shows the purpose, official publisher,
checked date, and an important limitation. This path does not promise that a
formal appeal is still available.

I prepare a draft. It remains "Your draft to review." Go Vermont has not sent
anything. The recipient, purpose, subject, and body stay separate, and I must
review both the recipient and wording before any contact control appears.

Assistant sharing is off by default. When I turn it on, the page exposes four
narrow WebMCP tools. `get_handoff_state` reads only the case I approved.
`find_civic_paths` returns the immutable official path and its limitation.
`propose_case_update` can stage clearer wording and unanswered questions, but it
cannot replace accepted text or change facts, sources, town, category, or
recipient. `prepare_handoff` stages the same deterministic draft for the path I
already selected.

A revision check rejects stale tool calls. Turning sharing off removes every
tool immediately. Only I can accept wording, copy the body, or open my email
application, and the resident's message never appears in a URL.

WebMCP makes the boundary useful: the assistant can collaborate inside the
source-backed contract, while the resident keeps every consequential choice.
